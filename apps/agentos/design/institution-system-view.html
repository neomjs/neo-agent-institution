System — the plane diagnosing and healing itself

Agent OS runs on self-diagnostics and self-healing. The diagnostics daemon notices and classifies (ADR 0025); the recovery actuator takes one bounded action at a time (ADR 0026). This view shows that loop at work, the maintenance that keeps the plane healthy, and what it runs on. Nobody is asked to act: where healing stops, the view names the gap in the daemons that has to close. Observe-only by declaration.

v4 (2026-09-28, operator direction). The installed view clipped its content and led with detector vocabulary ("restart churn", "class · threshold 90% · 2 samples"): fields a writer emits, not answers a keeper needs. v4 leads with the healing loop, moves every raw field one step down into each container's detail, and brings the scheduler back inside System as maintenance now · waiting · next, reversing v3's split. Progress is shown only where the plane measures it.

1 · purposeOne screen that makes the plane's self-healing visible, and shows where it has to grow

The plane runs itself. The orchestrator schedules heavy maintenance behind one exclusive lease. Its diagnostics daemon classifies every container's health, and its recovery actuator heals inside a bounded envelope: one action, cooldown, re-observe, record. The view shows that loop: what was diagnosed, what was done, and whether it held. Where autonomy stops by design, as at a knob's cap, or has no path yet, the view names the daemon gap and its ticket. That gap is work for the maintainers of the daemons, not a request to the operator. The view is for our own Fleet Manager setup and team; onboarding another operator's plane is a separate journey.

2 · questionsWhat the operator and the peers ask it

Operator
  1. Is the plane healing itself? What it diagnosed, what it did about it, and whether that held.
  2. Is maintenance moving? What runs now and how far along it is; what waits and why; what comes next.
  3. What does it run on? Each container in one line, and the evidence one click down when I want it.
Peers (MX, through the Neural Link)
  1. Where does self-healing stop? A heal at its designed limit, or a condition with no autonomous path, is the next work on the daemons. It names its ADR section and ticket.
  2. Is the plane safe to lean on right now? Before a heavy query or a recreate: is an exclusive task running, is a store near its ceiling?
  3. Why is my work waiting? A deferred lane names the task holding the lease, instead of a silent delay.

3 · content & actionsRanked, and why each piece is present

  1. Self-healing leads, because it is the plane's own account of its health. Each condition reads as the loop: diagnosed → acted → now. A condition that healed says so and gets out of the way. A condition at the edge of autonomy says why the loop stops there, as when ADR 0026 §2.8 refuses a raise past the knob's cap by design, and names the daemon work that moves the edge, with its ticket. No item tells a human to act.
  2. Maintenance comes second, because the heavy lane decides what the plane can do next. Now shows the lease holder with its phase and measured progress, waiting shows what the lease is holding back, and next shows the candidates the schedule picks from, and the rule it picks by.
  3. Containers come last, as an inventory: name, state word and one plain line each. The detector fields (pressure disposition, class, thresholds, churn baseline, diagnosis records) are still here, inside each container's detail. They inform the diagnosis; they are not the overview.
  4. No verbs, and no asks. The view carries no restart, raise or fix, and no "decide" line. Healing belongs to the daemons. A gap in it is maintainer work, routed through its ticket, not an operator runtime task.

5 · interactionGlance, then one click, never a dead end

  • Glance: the three bands, in that order, at every width. They stack in a narrow band and never truncate a word; nothing scrolls sideways.
  • One click: a container row opens its detail in place, where the raw fields live, and the Maintenance heading links to the heavy-lane timeline (frame 2). Both work in frame 1. The disclosure is a native <details>, so it is keyboard-reachable and reads its state aloud.
  • Staleness is a stopped clock on the head, never a silently current picture (frame 3). With no snapshot at all, one reason stands for the whole view (frame 4). The timeline carries a text alternative, the task list it draws.
  • Peers read the same bands as data through the Neural Link; a peer never needs a screenshot to learn what the lease holds.

Decisions recorded

  1. Order: self-healing → maintenance → containers (operator, 2026-09-28: Agent OS is self-diagnostics and self-healing).
  2. No asks (section 3, item 4): where healing stops, the view names the daemon work and its issue.
  3. A container's word is its run state (serving, restarting, stopped). The diagnosis lives in the self-healing band, so a wrong diagnosis cannot paint a serving store red.
  4. Raw vocabulary one level down. Detector fields stay in each container's detail, framed by a plain sentence. The overview never shows a field the operator would have to decode.
  5. The scheduler lives inside System as maintenance now · waiting · next. This reverses v3's split: the heavy lane is the plane's own state, not a separate pane.
  6. No invented progress (the three encodings under frame 2). A bar fills only from a run's own counter, such as REM's chunk n of m. Inside a chunk, or for a task that counts nothing, the bar is hatched and the running time stands beside it. A sampled count, such as the 438 undigested among the latest 2,000, is a number with its bound, never a bar. No percentages of a whole, no ETAs.
  7. One picture, one time. The running view reads one snapshot, so every value shares its time. Frame 1 is composed from several reads and says so in its own head, not only in its caption.
  8. A misleading reading is not repeated. The overview says what happened: Chroma uses 11.3 GiB, not the 84% the diagnosis counts, and each backup bundle is written while the run fails one requirement, not the three codes the ledger derives from one exit.
  9. The proposed schedule is marked where it is used. The Next column is a proposed contract, and it lists candidates, not an order. The picker's choice between two staleness jobs needs the waiter ledger and each task's last run, and the plane publishes neither yet, so the view groups those jobs unranked beside the rule that will decide. Each job names the rank it competes on: priority-0 or staleness.

Implementation gaps → work

  • A published schedule (lease holder, phase and progress where measured, waiting reasons, order, next due): a Brain leaf to file when this design is accepted. It is the one contract the Maintenance band cannot exist without.
  • Memory counted with file cache (the diagnosis and the snapshot both divided raw usage by the limit): neomjs/neo-agent-brain#614, fixed in PR #615, which also publishes memoryInUseBytes for this view. The heal ledger's retention, which one repeating record can fill, is not yet ticketed.
  • Chroma past its cap: neomjs/neo-agent-brain#60, the open ticket on Chroma's cap and working set; ADR 0026 §2.8 leaves the next step to the corpus architecture.
  • CPU throttling as a fact: neomjs/neo-agent-brain#594.
  • The out-of-memory cause on Memory Core: neomjs/neo-agent-brain#610.
  • Backup health codes and probe misattribution: the two Brain defect-notes of 2026-09-28.
  • Institution: #240 (task sample data out), #247 (shared pane chrome). The System view's rebuild follows as successors under #13, one band per leaf, self-healing first.

Tokens consumed, nothing minted

  • Type and space: v3's five roles and space-1…4 only.
  • Color: the closed state set. A stop in self-healing takes the idle amber: the edge of what the daemons do, not a failure. A healed condition and a serving container take the ok green, the wedged red marks only a run that reported failure (frame 2), and a derived value or an illustration takes the limited violet.
  • Motion: only the work running now moves. Its lease block breathes, and a bar whose progress is not measured drifts. Both run on the theme's liveness period, --fm-motion-pulse (2,400 ms, Viewport.scss), and only under no-preference. Reduced motion stops both, and nothing else animates.

  3. The published schedule: a section of the deployment-state snapshot, or its own read?
