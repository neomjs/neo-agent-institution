Catch Up — what changed while you were away, and where it leads

Catch Up is the briefing for someone coming back: the work that landed and the decisions made since they last looked, each one step from its evidence. It reports; it does not rank, decide or ask. When the reader is done, they close the window themselves, and the next visit starts there.

v2 (2026-09-28, operator direction: purpose and content first, visual design after). The installed pane opened with a question (three period buttons), a technical label (query-time · not authority) and three action buttons over a mostly empty area. It treated every visit after a Fleet-service restart as a first visit, and its pull-request section read one repository without being able to sign in to GitHub. v2 leads with what changed, in plain words. It keeps one window per visit and puts the acknowledgement last, and it names the five source gaps behind today's empty pane.

1 · purposeOne job: "I was away. What happened that I should know, and where do I look?"

For the operator and for peers alike. A person gets it as a pane, and a peer gets it as the same objects through the Neural Link. Catch Up is not the live feed, the picture of now or the plane's health; those have their own views (section 3).

2 · questionsWhat a returning reader asks, in the order they ask it

The operator
  1. How long was I away, and what does this cover? The window, as a sentence.
  2. What landed? The work that merged, in the team's own titles.
  3. What was decided or reversed? Work closed without landing, accepted decision records, graduated Discussions.
  4. Why does it matter? Catch Up leads to the reason and does not state it: each change opens its pull request, and the ticket it resolves says why. It writes no reason of its own, and the summary is context, not the reason (in Frame 1 it misnames two fixes). Reading each ticket's own title as a record is gap 5.
  5. Where do I look? Every change opens its pull request, which names its ticket, and a cited memory opens in the Memories pane.
A peer, through the Neural Link
  1. The same five questions, answered by the same stores: a window with its basis, each band with its state and coverage, items with canonical ids.
  2. How complete is this? A peer must tell "nothing happened" from "a source could not answer", so every band carries its state (the compact answer below Frame 4).

4 · orderWhy the pane reads top to bottom as it does

  1. The window comes first, as a sentence: "Since you last looked · 24 hours". It is the one thing that frames everything below it.
  2. Landed comes next. It is structured and checkable, and it is what most returns are for. Dependency bots fold into one line, so today 8 changes read where 20 merges happened.
  3. Decided comes third: what was reversed, or settled without landing.
  4. The team's words come last. The summary is context, not a record. In today's window it misnamed what two merged fixes did and named a repository that does not exist. Placed below the facts, it cannot contradict them unnoticed.
  5. The close comes at the end. The reader acknowledges after reading, never before, and never by just opening the pane. It is offered only when every band read: a close across a failed read would start the next window past changes this one never showed (gap 6).

6 · journeysEvery state the reads can produce has one rendering

  • Returning, with a last look on record: since then (Frame 1).
  • Last look unknown, either a first visit or the plane restarted since the last one. The source cannot tell these apart, so the pane says what it knows: "This plane doesn't know when you last looked", shows the last day, and offers the source's own look-backs (a day, three days, a week) in one control. There is no question before content (Frame 2).
  • Partial: a band whose source failed says so in one line with the source's reason, and the other bands render. An unavailable source never reads as "nothing happened", and Done reading waits until it answers (Frame 2, today's plane).
  • Reading: one line under the head, and no bands yet. The source settles both reads before it answers, in one envelope, so the bands arrive together (Frame 3).
  • Quiet: the reads ran and found nothing, and the pane says so per band (Frame 3).
  • Unavailable: with no history read at all, one reason stands for the pane (Frame 3).
  • Narrow: the same order at 296 pixels (Frame 4).

Decisions

  1. Facts before the summary. Landed and Decided come from records; the summary comes last and says where it defers.
  2. One window, said as a sentence. The half-open pair from #20 stays, and the head reads it in words.
  3. No question before content. An unknown last look shows the last day and offers the source's own look-backs in one control. No arbitrary periods are added.
  4. Reading never marks. The close is explicit, at the end, and advances only to the rendered window's end, as #20 landed it. It is withheld while any band's source failed.
  5. Nothing is ranked. Each band is newest first. Dependency bots fold by who wrote them, which is a grouping, not a synthesis.
  6. Technical words leave the head. query-time · not authority becomes "A summary, not the record" on the one band it describes.
  7. The partition moves to the summary, the only source that partitions; pull requests stay org-wide.
  8. Kept from #20: source-owned states, citation bounds and census flags, links out only for canonical pull-request ids, the close's receipt. Dropped: the first-use question row, the top action row (Live activity and Mark became a link and the close; Refresh is gone, since the pane reads on open), and the per-source pill groups. The manifest hash stays in the source detail.

Gaps, each a Brain leaf to file when this design is accepted

  1. The last look survives a restart: persist the per-viewer anchor, today "runtime anchors only".
  2. Pull requests across the org: the history reads one repository per call, and Catch Up passes none.
  3. The plane can read GitHub: explore_pull_request_history cannot authenticate from our plane's Memory Core (degraded, 0 resolved, 17:36 UTC).
  4. A decisions source: accepted ADRs and graduated Discussions, as records.
  5. A change's reason and goal: the resolved ticket's own title and its epic, read as records. Landed could then show why a change matters in the ticket's words, and group by what moved instead of by time, still writing no reason of its own.
  6. A close that cannot skip a failed read: markFleetCaughtUp refuses a window rendered while a band's source was unavailable, and the source reports whether the close is offered. Today it records the rendered end regardless, and a later close advances the last look past it.

Also reported, not a gap in this design: the summary's accuracy (Brain defect-note, 2026-09-28).
